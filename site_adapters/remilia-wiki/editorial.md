# Remilia Wiki editorial supplement

This document specializes the public Wikitool authoring and review skills for Remilia Wiki. It is project policy, not generic MediaWiki behavior and not executable prose policy.

Companion documents: [research](research.md) for where to look, [style](style.md) for voice, structure and mechanics, and [review](review.md) for review findings and the coverage step.

## Mission

Remilia Wiki is two things at once. It is a general internet-culture wiki: memes, communities, sites, terms, people and works, each written on its own terms and as fully as the evidence allows. And it is Remilia's own first-party record of its culture and history, written in large part by the people who made it and lived it, like a game studio's or a fan community's wiki.

In both roles one of its purposes is to canonicalize. Much of this knowledge (terminology, concepts, lore, in-jokes, aesthetics, chronology, who made what) is oral lore that exists nowhere else in citable form, and the wiki is where it becomes the reference. Its citation bar is therefore well below Wikipedia's.

It is not a promotion site. The goal is not for every article to lead back to Remilia.

## Contributors

Wikitool is public, and anyone may use it to contribute. The person using it is a contributor: possibly a Remilia member, an artist, a longtime community member or a newcomer. Do not assume who they are, whether they hold a role at Remilia or on the wiki, or that they are the subject of the article; do not infer identity from a name, handle or topic. If their relation to a subject matters to the article, ask.

The contributor is a source to work with, not a witness to be qualified. What they write or supply as fact goes into the wiki's own voice:

- Their own work, credits, roles, decisions and intentions. Write "She designed the guestbook", not "by her account, she designed the guestbook".
- Their knowledge of internet culture, Remilia and its neighboring subcultures: what a term or concept means, how a meme or practice originated, what a work or event was, who made what. When the contributor says "network spirituality means …", the article says "Network spirituality is …", not "according to the contributor, network spirituality is …".
- Their drafts and suggested wording. Keep it, and build the article around it.

Corroborate with citations where a public artifact exists (a post, credit, release, commit, log, scan, essay or announcement) and where it helps the reader. Never withhold, weaken or attribute a statement merely because no outside source repeats it; the absence of an outside source is the reason the wiki exists, not a defect in the claim.

Question a supplied statement only when there is a real reason: it is implausible on its face ("dogs are fish"), it contradicts an inspected source or another supplied statement, or it makes an allegation about an identifiable person or outside organization (misconduct, legal or reputational claims). Then ask the contributor for the basis before writing, and write what is resolved. Do not convert doubt into hedged prose. Promotion is the other limit: state what was made, done or meant, and leave praise and claims of outside importance to sources that establish them.

Write neither direction by default. Do not weaken a contribution to protect the wiki, and do not strengthen it beyond what the contributor said. Submissions pass through the wiki's moderation queue, and Remilia's admins adjust voice, scope and confidence there; the agent's job is an accurate, plain rendering of what the contributor supplied.

## Article object and framing

Define every subject on its own terms. A page's presence on Remilia Wiki does not make Remilia, Charlotte Fang, Milady Maker, Miya or "the community" part of the subject's definition.

Keep every real relationship to those entities, including influence, lineage and aesthetic connections, as plain sentences in the body. A relationship is real when an inspected source establishes it or a contributor supplies it. The agent does not infer one from resemblance, shared vocabulary or its own recall, and does not go looking for one to add. Cut a relationship only when it is shown wrong.

Placement keeps the article about its subject:

1. The lead names Remilia only when Remilia is part of what the subject is: Remilia made it, ran it, or it is defined by its role in Remilia's work.
2. The relationship sits where its topic is discussed. There is no "Relation to Remilia" section, and the relationship is not the article's closing paragraph.
3. Say what the relationship is: who did, said or made what, and when. "Adjacent" names none.
4. `Category:Remilia` and its subcategories mark Remilia's own work, not subjects that have a tie to it.
5. Do not use Charlotte Fang as a prestige anchor. If the relationship merely explains why an editor encountered the subject, keep it out of article prose.

Rules 1, 2 and 4 do not apply to articles about Remilia's own work. Rules 3 and 5 apply everywhere.

Remilia Wiki preserves niche and early material that lacks broad secondary coverage. A short, honest article or redirect is better than an inflated standalone page.

## Sources and evidence

This section replaces the public `wiki-writing` and `prose-review` skills' source-support thresholds, including the higher threshold they set for sensitive claims, on Remilia Wiki. Their other disciplines stay: read a source before using it, keep notes, verify quotations against the source, and bind review to the exact bytes.

Remilia-controlled websites, accounts, repositories, announcements, contracts, and artifacts, and the knowledge supplied by Remilia's members and the wiki's contributors, are primary sources and the authority for Remilia's own history and culture: releases, credits, roles, intentions, terminology, concepts, lore, technical properties, events, and dated announcements.

An editor's or contributor's own knowledge is a source. So is a tweet, a Reddit comment, a Discord message or an archived thread, on the same footing as a journalist's article: no kind of source is treated as more truthful because of who published it. Citations are welcome and help readers find more, but a claim without one is not a defect. The wiki's admins settle disputes about what is true.

### Provenance

Uncited is not unsourced. Every statement in an article comes from somewhere a person can point to: something the contributor supplied (notes, a draft, an answer to a question) or a document that was opened and read for this article. Nothing comes from the model's memory. A recalled fact is a lead for a search or a question for the contributor, never a sentence.

This is the one rule that the low citation bar depends on. It is what separates lore from invention, so it does not relax when a claim is ordinary, plausible or flattering. Keep the draft's notes able to answer "where did this come from" for every statement that carries no citation.

### Lore

Lore is knowledge held by the community that no article records: how a meme began, what a phrase meant, who did what in a thread. Write lore as a plain declarative sentence, with a citation if one exists and without one if none does.

Never use an attribution frame. Do not write "community accounts hold that", "is said to", "according to users", or "is often claimed". The frame hedges a statement the article is willing to make. If two accounts of the lore conflict and one is the contributor's, ask the contributor first, as [Contributors](#contributors) describes. State what remains disputed once, with who disputes it.

### What to cite

When there is something to cite, cite the thing closest to the event:

1. The artifact itself: the post, thread, page, commit, contract, recording or release, archived where it can vanish (see [research](research.md)).
2. Reporting and writing about it: news, books, academic work, and established blogs and podcasts.
3. Reference sites: Know Your Meme, Wikipedia, Fandom wikis, and Urban Dictionary. Use them for dates and leads, then follow them to the work they summarize and cite that.

This order ranks what to cite. It does not rank what may be said, and no statement waits for a higher item.

Cite the work rather than a summary of it. Never compress a Wikipedia article's many per-claim citations into a single Wikipedia citation or a generic publisher home page. Cite a summary directly only when it is the artifact under discussion or the only place the fact is written. When the work it summarizes exists but cannot be opened, cite the summary and say less.

Remilia Wiki pages are retrieval aids and claim trails. Inspect their underlying citations before reusing a claim. Do not cite the wiki as independent confirmation unless the wiki page itself is the primary artifact being discussed.

The adapter's source-review matches are reread signals, not automatic bans. Explain why a matched source is or is not suitable for the particular claim.

### Review

Review looks for claims that are wrong: contradicted by evidence the reviewer has, or by the page itself. It does not look for claims that are uncited, cited to informal sources, or cited to sources the reviewer could not reach. Cut or change a claim only when it is shown to be wrong.

Review reports three other things. A citation that says something other than the sentence it is attached to gets fixed, because it misleads the reader: move it to the claim it supports or correct the claim. A summary cited in place of the work it summarizes gets replaced. A statement whose origin the notes cannot show is reported as a question for the contributor, not cut and not hedged.

A reviewer never reports contributed content, or an allegation, for lacking attribution or a hedge.

## People

A claim about a living person has the same sourcing bar as any other claim. A contributor's knowledge is enough, and no kind of source is required. Two limits are not about sourcing, and they stay:

- **Origin.** The agent never originates a claim of misconduct, crime, or other reputational harm about an identifiable person or outside organization. Such a claim comes from the contributor or from a document read for this article. When a contributor supplies one, ask once for its basis, as [Contributors](#contributors) describes, and write what the answer resolves.
- **Privacy.** Do not publish the legal name behind a pseudonym, a home address, contact details, health, sexuality, or family details of a person unless that person has made it public or it is already established public record. This is not a sourcing rule: a true and well-sourced dox is still out.

A contributor's account of their own work and roles, and their account of Remilia's culture, are not sensitive claims, including on a biography of that contributor.

An allegation is written like any other statement: it carries attribution only where [Voice and register](#voice-and-register) would attribute any statement, and a missing attribution on one is not a finding.

## Voice and register

Write as an encyclopedia with gravitas: plain declarative sentences, concrete actors, actions and dates, and no defensive framing. Weight comes from facts, not adjectives.

- State established and first-party facts without hedges. "By his account", "reportedly", "is said to", "according to its author" and "has stated" belong only where the attribution itself tells the reader something.
- Attribute quotations, opinions, interpretations, and disputed accounts. When a passage rests on one account, such as a memoir or a single report, frame it once at the start of the passage and let the following sentences stand.
- Quote exactly, and as much as carries the meaning. Remilia's own writing often deserves direct quotation; a post's asides usually don't.
- Avoid stacked qualifiers, "it is worth noting", "notably", generic significance and legacy language, and paragraphs that close by restating their point. Judge artificial prose by the information it fails to deliver, not a word list.

## Reader value

Remilia Wiki is an encyclopedia, not a database dump or internal relationship map. Mechanical correctness does not rescue an article that a reader would not choose to read. Each paragraph should give a concrete understanding of this subject: identity, history, work, mechanism, chronology, terminology, reception, or a relationship.

Avoid padding thin evidence with broad NFT, internet-culture, art-scene, or Remilia background. Avoid generic significance and legacy language. Judge artificial prose through its information failure. The [style guide](style.md) lists words to watch as signals to re-read a sentence, not as bans.

## Structure and mechanics

Main-namespace articles use `SHORTDESC`, `Template:Article quality`, sentence-case headings, a References section with `Template:Reflist`, straight quotes, and wikitext rather than Markdown. These requirements are encoded in `site-adapter.toml`. Wikitool's lint enforces part of them; [style](style.md#mechanics) says which.

Pages a Remilia admin has accepted carry `{{Article quality|verified}}`; do not add visible "unverified" banners to them. Quality decisions on other submissions belong to moderation.

Use live template data and examples before filling an infobox. `parent_group = Remilia` is appropriate only for an actual Remilia project when the template supports it. Apply [category policy](categories.md) when selecting memberships or changing taxonomy; existing tags and retrieval suggestions are not approval.

## Acceptance

A named human must read and accept the exact final prose before Main-namespace promotion. Wikitool records a SHA-bound decision and invalidates it after edits. The editor field is self-reported and unauthenticated: it is an operational ledger, not identity proof. Agents must not enter a human identity without that person's actual review and instruction.
