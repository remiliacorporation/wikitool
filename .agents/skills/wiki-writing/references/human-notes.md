# Human notes to article

Human participation is an input to preserve and clarify, not friction to remove.

## Separate four layers

Keep the working record explicit:

1. **Supplied material** — documents, drafts, screenshots, transcripts, and links actually received.
2. **Human knowledge** — the person's own account, vocabulary, emphasis, uncertainties, and corrections.
3. **Verified evidence** — inspected sources that support publishable claims.
4. **Editorial decisions** — what to include, omit, attribute, qualify, or hold.

In the working record, do not relabel layer 2 as layer 3: note who supplied what. Whether layer 2 reaches the published voice as plain fact is a site-policy decision, covered below. Never discard layer 2 because it is not independently sourced.

## Asking for what is missing

Ask the person when the article needs knowledge that the supplied material and sources do not hold. Read what was supplied and reuse established scope first; an adequate brief, source-only research or a routine edit needs no interview. Follow the consequential gaps in their account: identity, chronology, firsthand versus secondhand knowledge, source locations, disputed relationships and exclusions. Ask in conversation, without a fixed questionnaire, and only what materially changes the article or its research.

Only the person's own words are testimony. Record answers verbatim with who said them and when. Never write an answer the person did not give, and label any placeholder or reconstruction as not testimony. Resolve whether ambiguous sensitive material is private, attribution-limited or excluded, and keep only the boundary future work needs.

Missing nonessential facts do not block a bounded draft: record them and continue. When the knowledge must outlast the session, keep it in an [interview ledger](interview-ledger.md).

## Preserve intent without preserving defects

Identify what the human is trying to communicate before restructuring it. Retain exact terminology when it is meaningful, but ask when a label is ambiguous, private, contested, or unusually capitalized. Preserve exclusions and do-not-assert boundaries exactly.

Mechanical conversion may normalize wikitext, citations, headings, and templates. It must not silently alter factual meaning, emphasis, uncertainty, or voice. Report material reorganizations and omissions.

## From firsthand knowledge to published prose

Keeping the four layers apart is a working discipline. It does not dictate the published voice; site policy does. Decide each claim's voice from who is speaking and what about:

- **First-party record.** When the site adapter treats the wiki as a first-party record of its own subject, the contributor supplying the material is a primary source, whoever they are, and the wiki is where their knowledge becomes the reference. Do not assume who they are or what role they hold, and do not infer it from a name, handle or topic. What they write or supply as fact is publishable in the wiki's voice: their own work, roles, decisions and intentions; the community's operations; and their domain and subcultural knowledge (what a term or concept means, how a practice, meme or work came about, what an event was). If the contributor says "the term means X", the article says "The term means X", not "according to the contributor, the term means X". Hobby, game, project and community wikis work this way. Cite a corroborating artifact where one exists (a post, credit, essay, commit, log or announcement), but do not require one, and never weaken the sentence because the source is the contributor. Where the site moderates submissions, voice and confidence are adjusted there; render what the contributor supplied, neither weakened nor strengthened.
- **Attributed account.** On wikis without that policy, attribute once and plainly (one framing clause such as "In her account of the project, …" followed by plain sentences), not with a hedge on every sentence.
- **Quotation.** Quote a person's exact words when the wording itself matters, and name the speaker; the quotation needs attribution, the surrounding facts do not.
- **Ask first.** When a supplied statement is implausible on its face, contradicts an inspected source or the contributor's other statements, or alleges misconduct or other reputational claims about an identifiable person or outside organization, ask the contributor for its basis before drafting. Write what the answer resolves; record an unresolved allegation as a hold. Doubt never becomes hedged prose.
- **Private context** stays out of publication.

First-party voice is not license for promotion. State what was made, done or meant; leave praise and claims of outside importance and influence to sources that establish them. What a contributor intended is fact; their verdict on the result (that it is legitimate, successful or important) is an opinion, so quote or attribute it.

Failure in either direction is a defect. Publishing an unexamined allegation about someone else launders it; hedging the contributor's knowledge with "by his account", "he has said" or "according to X" discredits the wiki's own record and reads as machine caution. Neither makes the article more accurate.

When a human draft is already good, edit locally. Do not replace it wholesale with generic prose merely to make it stylistically uniform.

## Human acceptance

The acceptance handoff must make visible:

- the exact final draft;
- which passages changed since the human's material;
- source limitations and unresolved holds;
- sensitive claims and their sources;
- the fact that any subsequent prose change invalidates the publication authorization.

The acceptance CLI records a self-reported editor claim. It does not authenticate the person and cannot prevent an agent from lying; workflow policy and human practice carry that boundary.
